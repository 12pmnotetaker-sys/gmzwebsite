Most of what goes wrong on a landscape project goes wrong before anyone digs.
A planting plan drawn without knowing where the water goes, a patio set at a
level that fights the house, a tree removed that needed a permit. All of it is
cheap to fix on paper and expensive to fix in the ground.

So the design stage is deliberately slow at the front. The first visit is spent
listening and looking: what you want the garden to do, and what the site will
allow. Then the ground gets read properly, dimensions and existing planting and
soil and drainage and the fall of the land, recorded rather than remembered.

What comes back is a concept to react to rather than approve, then a resolved
plan with costs against it and a plain account of what building it will
involve. On the Peninsula that usually includes permits, and more often than
people expect.

The consultation is a fixed price and covers the first hour on the property. It
is not credited toward later work, so what you are paying for is the visit
itself rather than a deposit on something you have not agreed to.

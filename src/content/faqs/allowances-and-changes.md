question: If the price is firm, why is there an allowance in it, and what if I change my mind?

A firm price is firm about everything the drawings can settle. A few things
cannot be settled on paper, and the usual one is plants: what the nursery has on
the day, in the size you want, is decided when you are standing in front of it.
So the price holds a named amount for that, and it says so. If what you choose
comes to less, you pay less. If it comes to more, the difference is priced and
you approve it in writing before anything is bought.

A change is the same rule applied to everything else. If you want something the
drawings do not show, it is written up as a change with its own amount and its
own effect on the payment schedule, and nothing on it is ordered or built until
you have approved it. The price you signed stays the price until you decide
otherwise, in writing, with the number in front of you.

---
question: Why do the drainage and the walls go in before anything I will actually see?
short: The order of the build
phase: building
serviceLine: design-build

published: true
---

Because everything else depends on them, and most of the cost of getting it
wrong is hidden. Drainage that was never solved shows up as a failed wall two
winters later. A patio built before the grading is settled has to come up again.

So the build runs the same way every time. Before anyone digs, the scope, the
sequence and the budget are walked through with you, so that surprises happen
in a meeting rather than in the ground. Then site preparation: clearing, grading
and drainage. Then hardscape, because planting around a finished wall is easier
than the reverse. Then the planting, placed for the light and the soil it will
live in; then irrigation laid out by hydrozone and lighting where it earns its
place; then any decks, pergolas or outdoor kitchens the project calls for.
Last, mulch, gravel and stone, and the site cleaned and the debris taken away.

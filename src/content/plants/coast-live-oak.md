A mature coast live oak is the oldest thing in your garden and the one piece of
it that cannot be replaced inside a lifetime. Most of what we do for it is
restraint.

Summer water is the part people get wrong. An established oak wants the ground
around it dry through the summer, and irrigation running against the trunk is
what kills them, slowly, over years. Two deep soaks across the season is
plenty, and the drip stays well away from the base.

Anything beyond clearance pruning and deadwood needs the city's permission
first. That is not our policy, it is the ordinance, and the permit reference is
on this record so you have it if you ever need to show it.

localNote: >-
  Portola Valley regulates the ground, not only the tree. A site development
  permit is required before fifty cubic yards of cut or fill, before a fill
  three feet deep or an excavation four feet deep, before an excavation or fill
  covering five thousand square feet, and before removing a significant tree or
  doing anything that would injure one, which expressly includes placing fill
  inside its dripline. Significant is set species by species and measured
  fifty-four inches above natural grade, with multiple trunks added together:
  eleven and a half inches across for coast live, black and valley oak and for
  bay laurel, seventeen for redwood and Douglas fir, and five for blue oak.
  Where a permit is granted the dripline is fenced before anything starts,
  trenching inside it is done by hand, no cut or fill is allowed within four
  feet of the tree's base, and the town can require a bond against damage. On
  most properties here the question is not whether a tree needs a permit but
  whether the earthwork does.
